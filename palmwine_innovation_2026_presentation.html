The Problem

Tradition should not
mean danger.

For generations, palm wine harvesters across Nigeria and West Africa have climbed tall palms using nothing but rope and carved footholds. The falls that follow cause injuries and deaths that do not make headlines.

"For generations, harvesters have risked their lives climbing towering palms. Our solution bridges heritage and technology."

Track 1  ·  Immediate

Mechanical Climbing Device

PALM-EASE 1.0 is a portable, foldable lift system that anchors to any palm trunk. A ratchet-lock or pulley-lift mechanism raises the harvester safely, with full harness support. No electricity. Under ₦180,000. Field-testable within months.

Track 2  ·  Long-Term

Bio-Engineered Palm Seedlings

Genetic modification of auxin and gibberellin pathways limits palm height to 3–4 meters. Embedded nanosensors monitor growth hormones and soil nutrients. Sap yield is maintained. Climbing becomes unnecessary.

05
Biotech Solution

Palms that grow only as high as your reach.

The long-term solution is to eliminate the need to climb at all. By modifying the auxin and gibberellin pathways that control vertical growth, bio-engineered seedlings stop growing at 3–4 meters. Embedded nanosensors monitor hormone levels and soil nutrients in real time.

"Imagine a future where the PALM-EASE device serves today's harvesters safely, while the bio-engineered palms mature to accessible heights, making climbing obsolete entirely."

Research begins with NIFOR and the UNN Biotechnology Department. Greenhouse trials follow in 2028. Field deployment targets Enugu and Anambra by 2030.

Sap flow and sugar content are preserved despite the reduced height. The palms also carry engineered resistance to drought and pests, making them suited for variable West African conditions.
