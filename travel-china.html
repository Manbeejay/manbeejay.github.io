Overview

A 10-day business trip in 2015, travelling with two partners. The airline was Ethiopian from Enugu via Niamey and Addis Ababa, landing in Shanghai. From there, a bullet train carried the group deep into Hunan Province, to Xiangtan, 湘潭, the base for most of the meetings.

The trip covered six companies in Xiangtan, a side trip to Jinhua in Zhejiang Province for three more, a return to Xiangtan, then a final stop in Guangzhou before heading home. Ten days, four cities, nine companies, and three bullet train rides that made every other train journey feel inadequate.

Three months after the trip ended, a delegation from the Chinese partner companies came to Enugu. The relationship built on this visit remains active.

Shanghai 上海

Day 1 · Arrival and overnight stay

The group landed in Shanghai, connected with the Chinese business partners, and stayed one night before the bullet train south to Xiangtan. Shanghai offered a few hours of city exploration: the Bund waterfront, the towers of Pudong across the river, and the particular energy of a city that moves as though it is always slightly ahead of itself.

First impressions of Shanghai

Arriving in Shanghai after a long flight through Addis Ababa, the city's scale registers immediately. The Bund runs along the Huangpu River with its row of colonial-era buildings on one side and the Pudong skyline, including the Oriental Pearl Tower and the Shanghai Tower, rising across the water. One evening was not enough, but it was a good introduction to what China was going to feel like.

Xiangtan 湘潭

Days 2 – 6 and Day 8 · Business base · Waltz Hotel

The bullet train from Shanghai to Xiangtan covered the distance in a fraction of the time any other mode of transport would have taken. Xiangtan is a prefecture-level city in Hunan Province, east-central China, historically significant as the birthplace of Chairman Mao Zedong, President Liu Shaoqi, Marshal Peng Dehuai, painter Qi Baishi, and scholar-general Zeng Guofan.

Xiangtan (湘潭) is a prefecture-level city in east-central Hunan province, south-central China. The hometowns of several founding leaders of the Chinese Communist Party, including Chairman Mao Zedong, President Liu Shaoqi, and Marshal Peng Dehuai, are under Xiangtan's administration. Source: Wikipedia.

Arrival and Accommodation

The group was received by their hosts on arrival and secured accommodation at the Waltz Hotel, a five-star property in the city. The hotel served as the base for the six days of meetings in Xiangtan.

Jinhua 金华

Day 7 · Overnight stay · Zhejiang Province

A one-day detour from Xiangtan to Jinhua in Zhejiang Province, to meet a third set of partners. Jinhua is a prefecture-level city of over seven million people, a major regional transport hub, and the city next to Yiwu, which runs the world's largest wholesale commodity market. Jinhua is also known as the source of Jinhua ham, a cured product with centuries of history.

Jinhua is a historically rich prefecture-level city in central Zhejiang Province. It is known as the production base for Jinhua ham and as the home city adjacent to Yiwu, site of the world's largest wholesale market. Source: Wikipedia.

  • Zhejiang Jirui Industry & Trade Co., Ltd. Security doors and related products

Guangzhou 广州

Day 9 · Final stop before departure

The last city before the return flight home. Guangzhou is one of China's largest cities and a major trading hub. The day included a meeting with the then president of the Association of Nigerians in China, a useful connection for anyone doing business across the two countries. Time was also spent in the electronics and general goods markets, which Guangzhou runs in quantity and variety that few cities can match.

Association of Nigerians in China

A meeting with the president of the Association of Nigerians in China provided useful context on the realities of doing business between Nigeria and China, the logistics, the relationships, the friction points, and the opportunities. The Nigerian community in Guangzhou is one of the largest African communities in China.

Tech and General Markets

Guangzhou's electronics markets are worth a separate visit on their own. Components, gadgets, finished goods, and everything in between, spread across multi-storey buildings in quantities that take time to absorb. Day 9 was the last day of ten, so the exploration was limited, but enough to understand why Guangzhou sits at the centre of so much global trade.
