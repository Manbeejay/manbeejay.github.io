Overview

A 10-day family holiday in 2013, flying out of Enugu with the family in tow: wife, son, and mother-in-law. The UAE delivered on every level. The plan split eight days in Dubai against one full day in Abu Dhabi, which was not a balanced split on paper but turned out to be the right one. Dubai at that pace rewards you; Abu Dhabi in a single concentrated day does too.

The UAE is a place that operates at the outer edge of ambition. The buildings are taller than they should be, the malls are larger than they need to be, the desert is right there behind everything, and the contrast between the two makes the whole experience feel slightly unreal in the best way. For a family with a child in tow, it worked extraordinarily well.

Emirates

Dubai

Days 1 – 8 · 8 nights

Eight days in Dubai is enough time to stop treating it as spectacle and start treating it as a place. The record-breaking buildings become background after a day or two. What stays is the texture of it: the souks in the morning, the fountain at night, the desert just outside the city limits, and the sheer variety of what a single place can contain.

Burj Khalifa & Dubai Fountain

The Burj Khalifa is the tallest building in the world and looks it from every angle. The view from the observation deck puts the scale of Dubai into perspective in a way that ground level cannot. In the evening, the Dubai Fountain performs in the lake below it, the water choreographed to music in sheets and arcs that reach 150 metres. Watching it from the waterfront after dark, with the tower lit above it, is one of those experiences that is both completely engineered and entirely genuine.

Dubai Mall

Two days inside the Dubai Mall is not excess — it is barely enough. The mall is less a shopping centre than a small city, with an indoor ice rink, a massive aquarium, a dinosaur skeleton in the atrium, and more retail square footage than most towns. For a family with a child, the variety kept everyone occupied and the food options covered every preference with ease.

Dubai Marina

Dubai Marina is the newer, sleeker side of the city. High-rises line a purpose-built canal full of yachts, and the waterfront walk connects one restaurant terrace to the next. The contrast with the old city is deliberate and complete. Evening dining here with the water and the lit towers alongside it was one of the more relaxed moments of the trip.

Gold Souk, Spice Souk & the Dubai Creek Abra

The old city sits on the Dubai Creek, and the contrast with the Marina could not be sharper. The Gold Souk's narrow covered lanes are lined entirely with jewellery — necklaces, bangles, and rings in quantities that seem impossible. The Spice Souk nearby fills the air with cardamom, saffron, and dried rose petals. Crossing the Creek on an Abra, a small wooden water taxi, costs next to nothing and is one of the best five minutes in Dubai.

Desert Safari

An afternoon desert safari was the single most memorable experience of the Dubai days. The dune bashing in 4x4s is exactly as alarming as it sounds and exactly as enjoyable. Camel riding followed, then a camp in the desert as the sun went down, and a barbecue dinner under a sky full of stars with no city light to compete with it. Taking the family into the desert and watching everyone react differently to the same vast quiet was something worth keeping.

Burj Al Arab, Jumeirah Beach & Palm Jumeirah

The Burj Al Arab — the sail-shaped hotel on its own artificial island — is one of those buildings that looks exactly like its photographs and still surprises you in person. Access to the interior requires a reservation, but the exterior and the approach road make for photographs worth keeping. Jumeirah Beach nearby is clean and wide, and Palm Jumeirah extends into the Gulf in a shape that only makes full sense from the air.

Abu Dhabi

Day 9 · Return day trip · approx. 1.5 hrs drive from Dubai

One full day in the UAE capital was enough to understand why people call it the more considered of the two cities. Abu Dhabi is quieter, more deliberate, and carries a cultural weight that Dubai's relentless momentum sometimes leaves behind. The day packed in more than seemed possible: the Grand Mosque, the Louvre, the Heritage Village, the Corniche, and Yas Island.

Sheikh Zayed Grand Mosque

One of the largest mosques in the world, and by any measure one of the most beautiful buildings in the UAE. The white marble, the 82 domes, the world's largest hand-knotted carpet inside the main prayer hall, and the courtyard that reflects the sky in its polished floors. Visitors of all faiths are welcome outside prayer times. The scale and the silence inside it are both unexpected.

Louvre Abu Dhabi

The Louvre Abu Dhabi sits on Saadiyat Island under a vast perforated dome designed by Jean Nouvel. Light falls through the dome in shifting patterns across the galleries below. The collection spans human civilisation across cultures and centuries, deliberately arranged to show connections rather than divisions. For a family, the building alone justifies the visit even before the art inside it.

Heritage Village

The Heritage Village on the Corniche reconstructs traditional Emirati life before oil: pearl diving, Bedouin camps, falconry, and the dhow-building trade that connected the Gulf to the world for centuries. A deliberate counterpoint to everything else Abu Dhabi has become, and a useful one.

Yas Island — Ferrari World

Yas Island holds Ferrari World Abu Dhabi, home to Formula Rossa — at the time, the fastest roller coaster on earth, reaching 240 km/h. The red Ferrari-themed dome that covers the park is visible from the highway. For a family with a young son, this was the highlight of the Abu Dhabi day and possibly of the entire trip.
